# Belgian Dark Ale on a Roasted Grain Bill — Variation 2: Spiced

**Same grain bill and hop schedule as Variation 1. The difference is the yeast and a paprika tincture added before kegging.**

T-58 brings a spiced, phenolic fermentation character — pepper, clove, dry finish.
A paprika tincture added on day 5 contributes a slow warmth on the back end that pairs naturally with T-58's own spice profile.
The result is darker and more restrained than it sounds: warmth rather than heat, spice rather than fire.

**~5.0% ABV · ~12 IBU · ~90 EBC · serve at 7–10°C**

## Paprika Tincture

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Paprika | 100 g hot paprika per 1 L of 96% ethanol |
| Maceration | 45 days |
| Addition volume | 50 mL per 50 L batch |
| Addition timing | Day 5 — one day before transfer to keg |

Added directly to fermenter. Stir gently or allow convection to distribute.

The tincture contributes warmth and a faint paprika (ljuta paprika) note on the finish rather than sharp heat.
T-58's phenolic spice character and the paprika warmth reinforce each other without either dominating.

## Notes

- 62°C saccharification rest vs 64°C in v1 — marginal but intentional: drier body suits the spiced profile
- T-58 selected specifically for its phenolic, peppery character as a complement to the paprika addition
- Tincture ratio (1g/L, 50mL dose) gives perceptible warmth without heat — adjust dose to taste if paprika AA% varies between batches
- No dry hop
- Same roasted grain bill as v1 — the spice layering reads differently against the roast than it would on a pale base

---

## Flavor Profile

- espresso and bitter chocolate
- dark dried fruit with phenolic depth
- pepper and clove from T-58 fermentation
- slow warmth on the finish from paprika tincture
- dry roast on the back end
- medium-full body, dry finish

---

## Style Notes

Same grain bill as Variation 1, different character entirely.

Where v1 (BE-256) reads as dark Belgian fruit ale, v2 reads as a spiced dark ale with heat. The paprika is a background note, not a gimmick — it surfaces on the second or third sip and lingers.

Not a style. Just a beer.
