# Problem Statement

Washing 20-25 beer kegs per week by hand is tiring, repetitive, and inefficient.

The main ergonomic problem was holding each keg by hand during rinsing. The operator had to hold and angle the keg during the initial beer-residue rinse and again during the final rinse after the caustic cleaning solution. Together, these two rinsing stages took about 5 minutes per keg in an uncomfortable bent posture.

The original manual process used only basic equipment:

- hose connected to a mixer tap;
- keg opening key;
- water;
- 35% NaOH concentrate.

The manual workflow for the first keg was:

- open the keg;
- rinse the spear tube from beer residue with warm water, about 25 C;
- rinse the inside of the keg from beer residue with warm water, about 25 C, while holding and angling the keg by hand;
- add 11 liters of water into the keg;
- add 0.5 liters of 35% NaOH concentrate;
- close the keg;
- shake the keg to distribute the cleaning solution;
- place the keg on one side for 8 minutes, wetting more than 50% of the inner surface;
- while waiting, open other kegs, rinse beer residue, and rinse spear tubes;
- turn the first keg onto the other side for another 8 minutes;
- transfer the cleaning solution into the second keg;
- close the second keg, shake it, and place it on its side;
- rinse the first keg from cleaning solution for about 3 minutes while holding and angling it by hand;
- rinse the first spear tube;
- repeat the sequence for the remaining kegs.

The original manual process provided acceptable cleaning reliability for the intended production scale. Across about 20 tons of beer per year, flavor artifacts attributable to keg cleaning were found in only one keg.

The problem was therefore not poor cleaning quality, but the amount of repeated manual handling required to achieve it.

The main problem is not that keg washing is technically difficult. The problem is that each keg requires several minutes of awkward manual support during rinsing, followed by more lifting, shaking, waiting, pouring, and attention switching.

At 20-25 kegs per week, the rinsing posture alone adds up to roughly 100-125 minutes of uncomfortable manual holding every week.

The cleaning cycle also contains many short waiting periods between mandatory manual actions:

- rotating kegs;
- transferring solution;
- rinsing;
- opening and closing vessels.

These delays are too short for meaningful context switching to other work, but too long for continuous focused operation.

As a result, the process continuously interrupts the operator and becomes mentally exhausting despite its technical simplicity.

The goal of this project is to make regular keg washing less exhausting by reducing repeated manual operations while preserving:

- practical cleaning effectiveness;
- low equipment cost;
- maintainability;
- repairability;
- process transparency.

The project intentionally prioritizes:

- reduction of operator fatigue;
- simplification of repetitive workflow;
- reusable cleaning solution circulation;
- mechanical cleaning action through controlled flow;
- easy inspection and disassembly;
- use of inexpensive and commonly available components.

This repository documents the iterative development of a small-scale keg cleaning system designed around practical constraints rather than industrial brewery assumptions.
