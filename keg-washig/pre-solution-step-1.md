# Pre-Solution Step 1: Stationary Rinsing Stand

## Identified bottleneck

Analysis of the original workflow showed that the most problematic stage was not chemical cleaning itself, but the rinsing operations.

The initial beer-residue rinse and the final rinse after caustic cleaning both required:

- holding the keg manually;
- continuously changing its angle;
- supporting its weight in an awkward bent posture;
- maintaining attention during long non-productive water flow operations.

This stage combined:

- static physical load;
- repetitive handling;
- operator attention lock;
- poor workflow parallelization.

The rinsing process therefore became the primary ergonomic bottleneck of the entire cleaning workflow.

## Proposed direction

The proposed first solution step is a stationary rinsing stand.

The idea is intentionally simple:

- place the keg upside down onto a fixed support;
- direct a vertical water tube with a simple spray head into the keg;
- connect the system to the water supply;
- start the rinse cycle;
- use a mechanical timer;
- leave the keg unattended until the timer expires.

The goal is not full automation.

The goal is elimination of prolonged manual keg handling during rinsing operations.

This approach potentially allows:

- hands-free rinsing;
- reduction of static operator load;
- partial parallelization with other tasks;
- more consistent rinse geometry;
- reduced operator fatigue;
- simpler and more repeatable workflow.

## Step 1 boundary

This step intentionally focuses only on rinsing.

It does not attempt to solve the entire keg washing workflow at once. In particular, it does not yet require:

- caustic solution recirculation;
- pump-driven CIP;
- automated chemical dosing;
- automatic switching between cleaning stages;
- closed-loop process control.

The purpose of this step is to validate whether removing hand-held rinsing already solves the largest ergonomic bottleneck.
